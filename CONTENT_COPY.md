## Project 1: Operational Capacity Analysis

### Card title
Operational Capacity Analysis

### Card subtitle
Healthcare operations analytics

### Card summary
Analyzed anonymized scheduling, capacity, donation-mix, calendar, and weather information for a fixed-site blood donation center. The analysis suggested that weather effects were modest on their own, while weekend constraints and platelet-heavy schedules created more meaningful planning risk.

### Case-study executive summary
This project examined whether winter weather meaningfully changed scheduled demand and capacity pressure at a fixed-site blood donation center. Aggregated daily scheduling and capacity information was paired with categorized weather observations. The results were descriptive: weather-related differences were modest, but winter conditions could compound existing weekend constraints, and donation mix mattered more than total volume alone.

### Key findings
- Weather alone showed only modest directional differences in scheduled utilization.
- Platelet share varied more meaningfully than total scheduled volume.
- Weekends operated closer to reported capacity and therefore had less tolerance for variation.
- Winter conditions should be treated as an additional planning factor, not the sole driver of staffing decisions.

### Recommended action
Use targeted staffing and resource adjustments on constrained weekend or platelet-heavy days rather than making broad structural changes based on weather alone.

### Limitations
Single anonymized site, one-month window, scheduled appointments rather than realized turnout, descriptive analysis, and no causal claims. Use synthetic or safely aggregated values in public visuals when needed.

## Project 2: Heart Disease Risk Prediction

### Card title
Heart Disease Risk Prediction

### Card subtitle
Applied machine learning

### Card summary
Built a Random Forest classification workflow and adjusted the decision threshold to prioritize recall. On the held-out test set, lowering the threshold from 0.5 to 0.3 increased recall from 75% to approximately 90%, with 3 false negatives among 32 positive cases.

### Case-study executive summary
This exploratory machine-learning project studied the tradeoff between false negatives and false positives in heart-disease classification. After cleaning and exploring the data, a Random Forest classifier was trained and the decision threshold was adjusted to prioritize sensitivity. The project demonstrates threshold selection and metric tradeoffs; it is not clinical validation and should not be presented as a diagnostic tool.

### Key findings
- Accuracy alone did not capture the cost of false negatives.
- Lowering the decision threshold increased recall on the held-out test set.
- The improvement came with additional false positives, illustrating the practical tradeoff between sensitivity and precision.
- Model performance depends on the dataset and requires external validation before any clinical use.

### Limitations
Small held-out positive sample, observational dataset, possible sampling and measurement bias, no prospective validation, no external validation, and no clinical deployment.

## Project 3: Sega Portfolio Analysis

### Card title
Sega Portfolio Analysis

### Card subtitle
Strategic analytics and business intelligence

### Card summary
Cleaned and grouped a 1,400+ title dataset with Python, modeled it in Power BI, and used DAX measures to examine platform mix and user-score volatility. The analysis highlighted a shift toward PC and mobile releases and greater variability among PC titles.

### Case-study executive summary
This project combined Python data preparation with Power BI reporting to examine Sega’s historical software portfolio. The cleaned dataset was modeled for interactive analysis across platform, time, release volume, and user reception. The findings were used to frame a quality-assurance recommendation rather than claim an observed business outcome.

### Key findings
- The historical release mix showed a substantial shift away from proprietary hardware toward PC and mobile platforms.
- PC releases had a high ceiling but greater user-score volatility.
- Mobile reception was more consistently limited but less variable.

### Recommended action
Treat the PC portfolio as a higher-variance investment and prioritize strong pre-release quality assurance and portfolio monitoring.
